2031 Project: SCOMP Peripheral Design
A custom peripheral was designed in VHDL for the SCOMP processor system on the DE10 FPGA platform to control four independent hobby servo motors. The project emphasized hardware-software co-design, integrating digital logic design, system-level I/O management, and minimal assembly for testing.
  • Implemented a servo control peripheral capable of driving four motors simultaneously with step increments under 2°.
  • Designed pulse-width generation logic to translate CPU commands into precise servo control signals, ensuring safe operation within 0.6–2.4 ms ranges.
  • Extended the I/O decoder to map a dedicated address for the new device and route commands through the shared data bus.
  • Integrated the peripheral into the SCOMP schematic, with connections for clocking, reset, and I/O control signals.
  • Wrote test assembly programs to send commands, validate servo responses, and provide user feedback through switches and LEDs.
  • Verified functionality via oscilloscope at multiple test points, measuring pulse widths, resolution, and timing accuracy.

Relevant Coursework

ECE 3058 – Architecture, Systems, Concurrency and Energy in Computation
This course explores the organization of modern processors, including cores, memory hierarchies, and I/O subsystems. It covers performance and energy considerations, parallelism, and introduces OS concepts like threads and networks, with simulator-based assignments to analyze tradeoffs.
ECE 3030 – Physical Foundations of Computer Engineering
This course covers the physical principles of computation, including how digital information is represented, processed, and stored. It focuses on MOSFETs, CMOS inverters, circuit speed and energy, and major memory technologies like SRAM, DRAM, and FLASH, with insights into fabrication and emerging computing technologies.
ECE 3150 – VLSI and Advanced Digital Design
This course covers advanced digital design in VLSI, from architectural modeling to circuit simulation. It emphasizes CMOS technology, transistor-level gate design, and optimized sizing, with projects on adders, multipliers, and synchronized datapaths, addressing interconnect delay, floorplanning, and power distribution.
ECE 2031 – Digital Design Laboratory
This course offers hands-on experience in digital system design, covering combinational and sequential logic, FPGA implementation, assembly programming, and CAD tools for simulation, synthesis, and VHDL development.
ECE 2035 – Programming for Hardware/Software Systems
This course examines how hardware supports high-level languages and operating systems, using C and MIPS assembly to illustrate implementation, with lectures and projects developing skills in system-level software design, performance evaluation, and debugging.
ECE 2026 – Introduction to Signal Processing
This course introduces discrete-time signals and systems, covering sampling, aliasing, digital filters, Fourier analysis (DTFT, DFT, DFS), and the Z-transform. MATLAB-based labs apply these concepts to audio, image, and biomedical signal processing.

Discovery Project

Project Idea

For my Discovery Project, my initial idea was to take a simple digital design—a synchronous binary counter—and push it through the full back-end implementation flow. While front-end RTL design is common in coursework, the full physical design (Place-and-Route) and sign-off flow is less frequently explored, especially at an undergraduate level. My goal was to understand how an RTL design becomes a manufacturable layout and to gain hands-on experience with industry-standard tools such as Innovus for design and Xcelium for functional verification.

Project Progress

Using a PnR tool I completed:

  • Floorplanning and power planning
  • Clock tree synthesis
  • Routing of nets
  • RC Extraction

I iteratively refined timing constraints and routing to achieve clean timing with acceptable slack. To ensure the layout was manufacturable and consistent with the original logical intent, I performed several sign-off checks:

  • Connectivity Check: Verified that all nets were correctly connected in the layout, ensuring there were no opens, shorts, or missing vias after routing..
  • Post-layout Simulation in Xcelium: Reran functional tests to confirm timing-accurate correctness.

By the end of the term, I successfully validated that it functioned as intended.

Project Successes and Challenges

Successes

  • Achieved a fully routed and sign-off-ready layout for the counter design.
  • Understood the end-to-end design flow.

Challenges

  • Timing closure issues: My initial constraints caused hold violations after CTS. I needed several iterations to insert hold buffers.
  • Tool complexity: Need to learn the commands for different tools.
